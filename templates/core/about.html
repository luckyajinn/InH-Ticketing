Usage and privacy

What {{ APP_NAME }} is for, what it keeps, and who can see it.

What it's for

Requests to {{ DEPARTMENTS_AND }}.

  • Product complaints or reactions, or anything about product safety or quality: tell QA directly, not here.
  • Never put a password, PIN or recovery code in a request, a reply or a screenshot. Nobody here will ask for one, and the app warns you if it spots one.

What it keeps

  • Your name and work email, from your Microsoft 365 sign-in. {{ APP_NAME }} never sees your password.
  • If staff open a request for you (when you ask at the desk, say), it's yours like any other: you get the emails and see it with your own requests, and its history says who opened it. If you haven't signed in yet, they type your name and work email; your Microsoft 365 sign-in replaces them the first time you sign in.
  • Your requests, replies and attached files, and the history of changes to each ticket.
  • Emails you send to the help-desk mailbox, which become part of a ticket. An email it doesn't act on (an out-of-office reply, say) is logged with its sender and subject only.
  • A copy of each email it sends you about a ticket.
  • When the password warning appeared and what you chose, but never the text itself.

Who can see it

  • You see your own requests and the replies to them.
  • Each team's staff see the requests for their own team, plus their internal notes and each ticket's history. Admins see every team's. The teams: {{ DEPARTMENTS_AND }}.
  • Admins also see reports made from the requests: counts and times per team, never figures for one person.
  • Internal notes and history are never shown or emailed to you.

How long it's kept

    {% if auto_close_days %}
  • A resolved request closes by itself after {{ auto_close_days }} day{{ auto_close_days|pluralize }} if nobody replies.
    • {% endif %}
  • A closed request is archived: it can still be read, but not changed. Archived requests are kept for two years.
  • The server keeps nightly backups for 14 days.

Cookies

Only the ones it needs to work: one keeps you signed in until you close the browser (12 hours at most), one protects forms from being sent by other sites, and a short-lived one carries notes like "Request sent" to the next page. No tracking, no analytics.
