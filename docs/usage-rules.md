**InH-Ticketing is for requests to IT, Mechanics, and Cleaning &
sanitization only.** These rules come from the system's intended-use and risk
assessment, reviewed with the compliance lead. The company is FDA-regulated.
A ticket holds a request and the conversation about it, never a record that
FDA rules require: those stay in the quality systems and logbooks. That is
what keeps the help desk outside FDA record-keeping rules.

## Use it for

**IT:** anything you need from IT, for example:

- a laptop, monitor, printer or phone that isn't working
- installing or updating software
- Wi-Fi, VPN or internet problems
- sign-in, password or MFA problems
- equipment or accounts for a new starter

**Mechanics:** a machine that won't start or has stopped; a leak, unusual
noise or vibration on equipment; a facility repair (a door, lighting,
plumbing or HVAC).

**Cleaning & sanitization:** a spill or mess to clean up; a room or area that
needs cleaning and sanitizing; cleaning or sanitizing supplies running low.

A Mechanics or Cleaning & sanitization ticket **asks for the work**. The record
that the work was done (a maintenance or calibration record, a cleaning or
sanitization record, a line clearance) is made where it always has been, never
only in the ticket.

## Don't use it for

| Not for | Use instead |
|---|---|
| Product complaints, reactions or anything about product safety or quality | Tell **QA** directly |
| Changes to Deacom, laboratory or quality systems | The **change control** process (a ticket can quote the change number for the IT work) |
| Approving someone's access to Deacom or other regulated systems | The **formal access request** process (a ticket can quote the approval for the IT work) |
| Batch, production, laboratory or quality records | The relevant quality system |
| Maintenance, calibration, cleaning or sanitization records | The usual logbook or quality system (a ticket can quote the record's number) |
| Customer or supplier communication | The usual business channels |

**Never put a password, PIN or recovery code in a ticket or screenshot.** No
team will ever ask for one.

## For staff: when something out of scope arrives

These apply to IT, Mechanics and Cleaning & sanitization staff alike.

| It's… | Do this |
|---|---|
| A product complaint or reaction report | **Forward it to QA the same day.** Reply to the person saying QA has it, then close the ticket with an internal note "Redirected to QA on <date>". **Never delete it.** |
| A request to change a regulated system | Ask for a change-control number; do the IT work only once it exists, and quote it in the ticket |
| A request for access to a regulated system | Check the approval exists in the formal access process; quote it in the ticket |
| Work that needs a maintenance, calibration, cleaning or sanitization record | Make the record in the usual place, and quote its number in the ticket |
| A ticket containing a password | Tell IT straight away: IT resets that password and tells the person, and an admin takes it out of the ticket with **Remove…** (the removal is logged). The ticket's history shows **Possible password** when someone sent one anyway after the warning, or emailed one |
| A request for another team in the app | Use **Wrong department?** on the ticket to ask an admin to move it |
| Anything else that isn't for your team | Point the person to the right team and close the ticket |

Internal notes are seen only by the team working the ticket and by admins,
never by the requester. When a ticket moves to another team, a note goes with
it only if it was marked to share. Write them as you would any company record:
factual and professional.

## How long tickets are kept

A resolved ticket closes 7 days later. A closed ticket is archived (read-only)
for two years, then due for deletion. Automatic deletion isn't switched on
yet. That's another reason a ticket must never be the
only copy of a record that has to be kept.

## Changes to these rules

Any new use of InH-Ticketing must be reviewed with the compliance lead
**before** it starts. That includes **adding a department** in the admin site
(Tickets → Departments): review first, then add it. Review these rules once a
year, and whenever the use changes.
